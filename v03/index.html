Sunday, the 5th of Octoberwritten on the front steps, coffee going cold

Hi, neighbor.
Here's what's around this week.

This is Stoop. It's just a board for the people who live near you: things to give away, things to sell for a fair price, rooms opening up, small jobs that need doing. No fees, no feed, nothing deciding what you ought to see. Everything here is in the order it was left, newest first.

If something catches your eye, write to the person directly. If you have something to pass along, there's room for your note at the bottom. Take your time.

Just start writing. The letter will quietly fold to fit.

Or wander by kind: everything, free things, for sale, rooms & apartments, jobs & gigs, help offered, and goings-on.

Free, for whoever needs it

A box of mason jars, maybe thirty of them, some with lids, sitting on the bench outside 412 Fern. Free. My canning era is over. Logan Square — Ruth write to Ruth

Our son outgrew his wooden balance bike. One small scuff, otherwise loved gently. Free to a family who'll use it before the snow. Ravenswood — the Okafors write to them

A pothos cutting in a yogurt cup, already rooted. I have eleven. Please, take one. Free. Pilsen — Marisol write to Marisol

For sale, at a fair price

A solid oak bookshelf, five shelves, six feet tall. Heavy enough that you'll want a friend. It held my grandmother's encyclopedias for forty years. $60. Andersonville — Theo write to Theo

A Schwinn three-speed, mint green, new tubes and a basket that squeaks only on left turns. $145, or a fair trade for a good stand mixer. Humboldt Park — Dev write to Dev

An Olympia typewriter that still clacks, with a fresh ribbon and the original case. It wrote at least one bad novel. $90. Hyde Park — June write to June

Two cast-iron skillets, 8 and 12 inch, well seasoned. Moving somewhere with a tiny kitchen. $25 for both. Bridgeport — Sal write to Sal

A small thing: meet in daylight, somewhere with other people around. The library steps on Lincoln are good. So is the coffee place with the dog that sleeps in the window.

Rooms opening up

A sunny room in a two-flat, with a bay window and a radiator that sings in the morning. Shared with one quiet nurse and one loud cat. Available November 1st. $825 a month, heat included. Ukrainian Village — Priya write to Priya

A garden-level one-bedroom with its own door and a little patch of tomatoes you're welcome to keep going. Laundry in the basement, quarters not required. $1,150. Albany Park — Mr. Kowalski, landlord, 2nd floor write to him

Work, small and otherwise

The bakery on Clark needs early hands, 5 to 9 a.m., Thursday through Sunday. No experience needed, only punctuality and a tolerance for flour. $18 an hour, plus bread. Edgewater — Lina at Rise write to Lina

Looking for someone to walk Biscuit, an elderly beagle who mostly wants to sniff one particular fence, weekday afternoons. $15 a walk. Lincoln Square — Harold write to Harold

Help, offered

Retired piano teacher with an open Tuesday. Patient with beginners of any age, including the ones who say they're too old. $30 a lesson. Rogers Park — Mrs. Abernathy write to her

I'll hem your pants or fix a zipper while you wait on my porch. Lemonade until it gets cold, then tea. $8 to $20. Pilsen — Rosa write to Rosa

What's going on

A block-wide stoop sale on Saturday the 11th, 8 a.m. until we get bored. Bring a table, bring a neighbor. Free to join. Wolcott, between Leland & Wilson say you're in

Did anyone lose a grey knit hat with a little felt fox on it? It's on the fence post by the Laundromat, waiting. Avondale — a passerby

Nothing like that this week, I'm afraid. Things come and go, so check back on Thursday, or leave a note saying what you're hoping to find. Someone might have it in their basement.

It takes about a minute. Nobody will charge you for it.
Thank you. Your note is on the stoop now.

That's all for now. Be good to each other, and if you borrow a ladder, bring it back.

Warmly,your neighbors at Stoop

P.S. We'll never sell your name, and there's no feed here to keep you scrolling. When you've read to the bottom, you've read everything. Go outside.
